His signal dies. This is where every other app of this kind goes blank. His doesn't: before he left, the system handed him a message small enough to ride a satellite segment, and his phone kept it. He can still ask, still hear the answer, still see the zone.

The current pushes him east. The phone speaks without being asked: at this heading you cross the Sri Lanka maritime line in 34 minutes, turn to 090. That sentence is the difference between an ordinary day and a seized boat with the crew in custody.

The problem

Ocean data exists. Understanding doesn't.

ISRO and global agencies publish enormous volumes of satellite and oceanographic data every day — sea surface temperature, chlorophyll, wave forecasts, cyclone tracks. A fisherman deciding whether to sail tomorrow cannot query a NetCDF file. Existing tools either push raw advisories or answer one narrow question. None reason across sources, in the user's own language, with the evidence attached.

Fishermen

Where is it safe? Where are the fish?

Maritime operators

Safe routes, boundary awareness.

Coastal authorities

Disaster response, hazard alerts.

Researchers

Explainable trend attribution.

The brief. Build an agentic conversational platform that decomposes natural-language questions, coordinates specialist agents across heterogeneous marine datasets, performs spatial-temporal reasoning, and returns explainable, evidence-backed answers — in the user's own language, with proactive hazard alerts and geofencing near restricted waters.

What separates this

Five decisions, each demonstrable in ninety seconds

The default reading of this problem is a cloud chatbot over ocean APIs with a map. That stops working fifteen kilometres offshore, which is exactly where the user is.

Offline

Works with zero network

Agents reason on a server; the conclusion is encoded into a 145-bit capsule that fits one NavIC L5 segment. Scan a QR — or take it over Bluetooth or SMS — in airplane mode and the phone reconstructs a full spoken advisory from a codebook it already has.

Trust

The numeric firewall

The model narrates, but every figure it writes is checked against an evidence bundle before it ships. A fabricated wave height cannot reach the screen — the string is replaced by a template built only from real values.

Safety

Predictive boundary ETA

Not "you crossed the line" but "at your present heading plus 0.8 kt of set, you reach it in 34 minutes." Drift-projected, carried as an 8-bit field inside the capsule, so it still fires with the network down.

Science

Reasons, doesn't retrieve

"Why has productivity declined here" needs correlation across chlorophyll and SST trends, not a lookup — the hardest of the eight official queries, and the one that proves a multi-agent architecture earns its complexity.

Honesty

Candid about its own limits

Boundaries are labelled indicative, never legal. PFZ is scraped and attributed, never implied to be a live feed. Stale data shows its age instead of being served as current.

Access

Vernacular by default

Detects the language of the question and answers in it, starting with Tamil and Hindi and architected for Bhashini's full set. Colour, icon and spoken audio carry the verdict together, so literacy is never required.

Signature mechanism

The trick is the reason code

The server decides why and sends an 8-bit index. The phone already holds 256 explanation templates in twelve languages and renders the sentence locally. A full spoken Tamil advisory costs the link one index, not a paragraph.

Every quantiser clamps. A 9 m sea saturates at the top of the scale and still reads as dangerous; wrapping it would read as calm, and a crew acts on calm.

The bits are identical on all three. That is the point: the NavIC claim is segment conformance, and conformance is arithmetic that doesn't care which pipe the bytes arrived down.

We don't need the satellite to prove the satellite claim. The uplink is ISRO's, so no team here transmits on it. The demonstrable claim was never "we broadcast" — it is "our advisory fits the segment ISRO already broadcasts on." That's arithmetic, and arithmetic is checkable on the ruler above.

The approach

A planner, six specialists, and a rule the model can't break

Exposure

What it can't do, said before you ask

A judge who finds the edge you hid trusts nothing else you claimed.

The offline envelope

With no network the app answers from the last capsule, cached fields and the phone's own GPS: the verdict, the numbers in the capsule, the fishing-zone bearing, boundary time against live position. It cannot answer a new free-form question about a patch of sea that was never fetched.

Offline figures are quantised

Wave to 0.25 m, temperature to 0.5 °C, current to 0.1 kt, every bearing to the sixteen-point compass. That is the cost of fitting a segment, and the card says so rather than implying a precision the bits don't carry.

Boundaries are indicative

Every line is drawn from a best-available compilation, never a legal delimitation, and the database refuses to store one marked otherwise. Distances are geodesic metres, not degrees.

Thresholds are provisional

The safety numbers are engineering placeholders pending the published INCOIS Ocean State Forecast criteria. Every assessment carries that flag, so nothing can present them as official by omission.

Broadcast is the scaling story. One capsule per zone serves every boat in that zone, so cost grows with the number of zones, not the number of users. A chatbot costs one inference per question per user; this costs one encode per zone per cycle.
